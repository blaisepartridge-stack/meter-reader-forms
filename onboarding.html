Hours & Work Requirements

Contract meter readers are expected to work a 30-hour week and minimum of 6 hours each day, Monday to Friday. This excludes all public holidays. Saturday work is offered on occasion depending on the status of the cycle schedule. Work hours are capped at 10 hours per day, and 42 hours per week.

Work time starts when you arrive on site and start looking for your first meter. Work time ends when you read your last meter. Commutes between segments is paid time, however you must complete at least one hour of work if you move onto a second route. It is a requirement of the role to travel to areas outside of your immediate locality and occasionally across the river if requested. If you are unable to complete your minimum hours or finish your current segment, you must contact your designated scheduler.

Hours of work vary throughout the year and are dependent on the season. In the Summer months you have the flexibility to start each morning between 6AM and 9AM, and finish before 6PM. In the Winter months, you have the flexibility to start between 7AM and 9AM, and finish before 5PM. These seasonal changes will be communicated as necessary. If you need to start after 9AM or call in sick, you must call your designated scheduler.

Work is required in all weather conditions. We abide by a strict two-month billing cycle and cannot afford the luxury of taking days off due to extreme heat or rain.

A mandatory break of 30 minutes must be taken after 6 hours of continuous work. If you fall short or are on par of this quota, you aren't required to take a break. You are entitled to a 15-minute paid break; however, this does not count toward your 30-minute mandatory break.

To request leave or time off, please email our office staff. As water meter reading is a casual position, we are accommodating of leave requests. However, we would ask you to give as much notice as possible as it will have an impact on cycle and schedule deadlines.

Performance Expectation & KPI's

As a meter reader your performance will be measured on obstruction rate, productivity, hours worked, and communication. Each meter reader will receive a performance review, one after two weeks and another after two months of employment. This allows us to identify areas of strength and weakness of each contractor's performance.

Obstruction Rate

Obstructions are reads that you haven't been able to obtain due to access issues or unforeseen obstacles. Every skip read you process (besides 'Gate' and 'Dog') are sent to an internal car to reader to follow up on. We expect the meter reading workforce to maintain an obstruction rate of 3% or less. Any higher than this will create further work and put added pressure on our internal teams to rectify.

Productivity

The productivity rating reflects your efficiency and is calculated based on the grade (A-E) assigned to a segment. A value has been assigned to each meter based on the grade of the segment. The sum of these values provides us with a productivity total which is divided by the number of hours spent completing the work to give us an indication of the reader's efficiency per hour. The expected productivity rate per hour across the Meter Reading workforce is 35.

For example:

You read 540 E grade reads in six hours.

(540 / 6) x 0.39 = 35 (productivity score)

This is an acceptable read rate and falls within our expected productivity parameters.

A productivity of 35 would mean you are reading 90 E Grades per hour, 75 D Grades per hour, 66 C Grades per hour, 55 B Grades per hour and 35 A Grades per hour.

It would also mean you are reading 540 E grades per day, 450 D grades per day, 396 C grades per day, 330 B grade reads per day, 210 A grade reads per day.

E = x 0.39
D = x 0.47
C = x 0.53
B = x 0.63
A = x 1

Hours Worked

As a meter reader, you are required to work a minimum of 6 hours per day. This is inclusive of commutes between rounds and toilet breaks. We would expect that the hours you raise through the Programmed GO Dashboard are almost on par with your Temetra reading hours. If there is a significant gap between the two, this suggests readers are exaggerating commutes between routes and taking extended toilet breaks. This can be seriously detrimental to the productivity of the cycle schedule.

Communication

Given the unsupervised nature of the role, we rely on and value effective and proactive communication from the Meter Reading workforce. If you make changes, leave work, and don't make yourself available to our office staff, this can be seriously detrimental to the productivity of the cycle schedule.

If you fail to maintain these KPI's you may be subject to performance review and further training.

General Breach Guidelines

To ensure our meter readers are safe in the field and follow correct work procedures, we require them to abide by the following guidelines. Violation of these guidelines will result in a breach. Accumulation of three minor breaches will result in a major breach. A major breach will result in an official written warning and may result in termination. A breach will go against your record and are valid for twelve months from anniversary date.

Minor Breach

  • Failure to wear issued PPE and use safety equipment as instructed.
  • Not having access to first aid kit in the field.
  • Use of earphones for recreational purposes in the field.
  • Not wearing ID card while working.
  • Failure to meet communication expectations.
  • Failure to follow correct work procedures in field.
  • Failure to accurately record and/or present vehicle logbooks.

Major Breach

  • Attending work while unfit for duties (intoxication).
  • Intentional non-compliance with safety procedures.
  • Not reporting an incident or near miss verbally ASAP or a reasonable time on the day with justification.
  • Misuse of equipment or company vehicle.
  • Intentional falsification of readings, hours worked, or related processes.
  • Non return of PPE, equipment, or asset within 5 business days, as requested by WC.

Guideline to the Line Manager to process this form Not active

Parts 1 to 9 of this form provide a basic level of information about a worker's driving experience and skills (conditions driven, vehicle types, infringements and vehicle incidents).

Use this information as a basis for discussion with workers, to have a clear understanding of their driving experience. Apply this information to determine if they are to be profiled to undertake driver training ('Defensive Driving' and/or 'Operate a 4WD' training).

The worker is required to undertake driver training if the Line Manager determines either that:

  • The worker has not had the equivalent of 12 months regularly driving a vehicle of similar type or in a similar environment to that involved in their job role, in the preceding 5 years, or
  • The worker lacks appropriate driving skills or defensive driving habits, based on their incident or infringement history or other information provided at part 8 above, or
  • The worker's job role requires them to drive on unsealed roads, and their 'Defensive Driving' or 'Operate a 4WD' training has exceeded 3 years.
